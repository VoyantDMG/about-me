Golf weekend: Saturday 97 to Sunday 78

My first round ever under 80, less than 24 hours after one of my worst. Four short stories about showing up, three strangers, and what the round was really about.

97Saturday
78Sunday
Part 1The comebackI almost skipped the round.

On Saturday I shot a 97, including a 10 on one hole. I went to the range afterward, frustrated, and seriously thought about skipping my tee time the next morning.

I went anyway. I got paired off the wait list with three strangers: a barefoot former Marine, a guy I'd played with once before, and a serious guy who'd randomly break into song.

Less than 24 hours after one of my worst rounds, I shot 78. The first time I've ever broken 80.

Even par on the front nine. The back nine got shaky with a penalty and a double on 16, then I answered with a birdie on 17.

My business has had its own 97. A while back, I got scammed out of $15,000. It stung, and it would have been easy to pull back and play it safe. Instead, I doubled down, tightened up how I vet partners, and kept building. The business has grown since.

A bad day isn't the verdict. Showing up the next day is the whole game.

Part 2Dan, unfilteredBarefoot, and a swim in the waterfall.

Dan showed up to the clubhouse in sandals. On the first tee, he kicked them off and played the whole round barefoot, spinning through his swing like a whirling dervish. Between 12 and 13, he took a swim in the waterfall. He played pretty well, too.

What stuck with me was how completely himself he was. No filter, no worrying about what anyone thought. It was contagious, and it probably loosened me up more than any swing thought on the way to my best round ever.

I'm working on putting myself out there more, and Dan is a good reminder that the people you remember are the ones who show up as themselves.

Who's someone who reminded you it's okay to just be yourself?

Part 3TJ and the MattsA round I never got to finish.

The first time I played with TJ, he brought his buddy Matt, from Long Island. I'm from Long Island. And almost every one of my best friends, including my brother, is named Matt or something that rhymes with it. Some people collect golf balls. I apparently collect Matts.

That day, I was in the middle of one of my best rounds ever when I had to walk off on 16 to pick my wife up from the airport. This weekend, I'd just dropped her off at the airport, got paired with TJ again, and finally finished the job: my first round under 80.

What's the strangest coincidence that led to a great connection for you?

Part 4What the round was really aboutThe 78 is the least interesting part.

Looking back on the best round of golf I've ever played, the 78 is the least interesting part.

What I remember is everything around it. I almost didn't show up after shooting a 97 the day before. I got paired with three strangers off the wait list. Dan played barefoot and jumped in a waterfall. Kevin, the serious one, would suddenly break into song. And TJ, who I'd met once before on a round I never got to finish, was there when I finally broke 80.

None of that happens if I stay home and stew over a bad round.

That's the lesson I keep coming back to, on the course and in business. The breakthroughs don't come from the perfect plan. They come from showing up, being yourself, and staying open to the people you meet along the way.

I connect people who need help with the providers who can help them. My company delivers inbound calls, live transfers, and leads for debt, insurance, financial services, and home services providers, with a focus on real intent and quality over volume.

Behind every call is a person dealing with something real: a debt they can't get ahead of, a family that needs coverage, an AC that quit in the middle of a Texas summer. I never lose sight of that, and it shapes how I run my business.

I'm happily married, based in Texas, and originally from Long Island. I believe common sense and honest conversation beat picking sides.
